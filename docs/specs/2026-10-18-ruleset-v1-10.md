# Ruleset v1.10: black, yellow, purple, minimum buy round

Status: draft for the owner's approval. Date: 2026-10-18.

## Intent

v1.8 changed how points are earned. v1.9 added spellbook 1 with new blue, red and orange 6. v1.10 is the last colour step before v2 (issue #79). It adds three colours (black, yellow, purple) and one small mechanic: a colour can be bought only from a given round on. v2 then adds random round-start cards; that is a later spec.

v1.10 also becomes the default ruleset of the code. The owner decided this on 2026-10-18 (issue #128 is closed as decided, with v1.10 as the default instead of v1.9).

## Goals

1. Ruleset `v1.10`: spellbook 1 with the colours of v1.9 plus black, yellow and purple, and a minimum buy round per shop item.
2. Bots, record and replay, CLI and the RL environments run on v1.10 (no retrain).
3. v1.10 is the default `rules` value of the code.
4. The skill `quack_rule_changes` and the open doc findings of the stage review of #123 are brought up to date.

## Non-goals

- More spellbooks, more colours, balance work. The owner closed the issues that ask for them.
- Round-start cards (v2).
- Retraining a policy on v1.10.
- Changing any older version.

## Anti-goals

- Changing the behaviour of `v1`, `v1.1`, `v1.8` or `v1.9`. Their games, records, datasets and tests stay valid.
- A hidden hand leaking, a hard-coded colour count, or a new action inserted into the middle of the action list (known mistakes in the skill).

## Words

- Drop: the droplet position. "Drop +1" is two half steps (`droplet_halves += 2`), "drop +1.5" is three, "drop +0.5" is one. (The owner's word "drop"; my reading.)
- Pot: the chips a player has placed this round.
- Points: the normal game points of the player.

## New colours

All three move by their value first, like every chip. Spellbook 1 gets three entries; v1.9 has no chip of these colours, so v1.9 does not change.

| Colour | Values (price) | Buyable from |
|---|---|---|
| Black | 1 (10) | round 1 |
| Yellow | 1 (8), 2 (12), 4 (18) | round 2 |
| Purple | 1 (9) | round 3 |

### Black (round-end effect)

At round end, when both players have stopped, count the black chips in each player's pot. The count includes an exploded pot. Let `a` be the count of one player and `b` the count of the other:

- `a >= 1` and `a == b`: each of the two players drops +1.
- `a > b` (and so `a >= 1`): the player with the larger count drops +1.5. The other player gets nothing.
- Otherwise nothing.

The effect fires once per player, however many black chips there are.

### Yellow (placement effect)

When a yellow chip is placed, it moves by its value. Then, if the chip placed just before it in the same pot was white, that white chip goes back into the bag. The droplet movement of that white chip is not taken back. The explosion total goes down by the explosion weight of that white chip. The effect is the same for all three values of yellow.

My call: the effect runs before the explosion check of the yellow step, so a yellow chip can pull a white chip out and prevent an explosion in the same step. If the yellow chip is the first chip of the pot, or the previous chip is not white, nothing happens. After the white chip leaves, "the previous chip" of the next placed chip is the yellow chip.

### Purple (round-end effect)

At round end, count the purple chips in the player's pot (an exploded pot counts, my call, as for black and green):

- exactly 1: 1 point.
- exactly 2: 1 point and drop +0.5.
- 3 or more: 2 points and drop +1.

The purple chips are not used up. They go back into the bag like all other chips.

## Minimum buy round

A shop item has a minimum round. The item can be bought in a round only when the round number is at least the minimum. The rounds are numbered 1 to 9, as today.

- New ruleset field on the shop item: an integer, minimum round, default 1. Older versions keep the default and need no edit.
- A shop item below its minimum round is not a legal action. So the RL action mask, the bots and the CLI follow without an extra rule.

## Order of round-end effects

At round end, per player, in this order: the ruby (as today), green, black, purple. Green is the only effect that can fire twice, when both the last and the second last chip of the pot are green (as today). Black and purple fire once.

Green looks at the last two chips of one pot. Black and purple look at the whole pot, and black compares both pots. The existing round-end hook sees only the position of a chip; the builder widens it for the new effects in a way that keeps the old components and old versions unchanged.

## Defaults

v1.10 becomes the default `rules` value wherever the code has a default (the CLI, the tournament, the run and simulate functions, the RL environments). My calls:

- The test `tests/test_rules_defaults.py` today checks that no default is the newest version. It is changed to check that the default is v1.10, and to fail on a default that points anywhere else.
- Notebooks name their version in the notebook. They keep it.
- Saved data of older versions (games, runs, datasets) still names its own version and still loads.
- A trained policy cannot act on the new layout. Every entry point that runs a trained policy refuses v1.10 with a clear error, as for v1.9. The environments and the baseline bots work on v1.10. A policy-using command that is run without an explicit version now gets the default v1.10 and therefore refuses; its error says to name an older version.
- The skill text that says "no default may be the newest version" becomes wrong with this change. It is rewritten in the same stage.

## Game record and engine

- The effects of black and purple and the put-back of yellow are events in the record, so replay works. The record format is generic; no schema change is expected.
- A white chip that returns to the bag must be put back into the bag in the state, and the bag order or the chance log must still replay exactly.

## Bots

Every bot answers every action the engine offers. The heuristic bot: the shop strategies may buy the new chips (a strategy is a list of shop item ids), and its look-ahead for the explosion total accounts for the yellow put-back. The random bot needs nothing.

## CLI

The board and the shop show the three colours. Shop items below their minimum round are shown as not available or not shown. The buy keys must cover the bigger shop.

## RL environments

Observation layout 5 covers the new colours, the new shop items and the minimum rounds. The action list grows by the new shop items, appended at the end. The stored sample of layouts 1 to 4 is made first, before the encoder changes. No retrain.
