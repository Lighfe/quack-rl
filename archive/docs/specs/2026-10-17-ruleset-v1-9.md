# Ruleset v1.9: spellbook 1, new blue, red, orange 6

Status: draft for the owner's approval. Date: 2026-10-17.

## Intent

v1.8 changed how points are earned. v1.9 is the first colour step towards v2 (issue #79). It does two things:

1. A colour no longer holds its effect directly. The ruleset names a **spellbook**, and the effect of each colour is looked up there (issue #71). v1.9 defines spellbook 1. Later stages add spellbooks where the same chips have other effects.
2. Spellbook 1 changes blue, adds red and a second orange chip, and removes the shop item "Remove one White 1".

The stage is also a test of the project skill `rule_changes` (see "Testing the skill"). More colours and small game component changes come in later stages.

## Goals

1. Ruleset `v1.9` with a spellbook field. Effects of coloured chips come from the spellbook.
2. Blue, red and orange as below. Green and white keep their effects.
3. The shop has no "Remove one White 1".
4. Bots, record and replay, CLI and the RL environments run on v1.9 (no retrain), as they did for v1.8.
5. A fair test of the `rule_changes` skill, and a rewrite of the skill from its result.

## Non-goals

- More colours, more spellbooks, balance work.
- Retraining a policy on v1.9.
- Changing the points table, the explosion choice or the last-round rule of v1.8.

## Anti-goals

- Changing the behaviour of `v1`, `v1.1` or `v1.8`. Their games, records, datasets and tests stay valid and unchanged.
- A new default: no default `rules` value becomes `v1.9`, and notebooks stay pinned.

## Spellbook

A **spellbook** is a named set that says what each coloured chip does. A ruleset names one spellbook. A chip has a colour and a value. The effect of the chip (placement bonus, explosion weight, round-end bonus, and the new effects below) is looked up through the spellbook of the ruleset.

- White and orange do not depend on the spellbook: they work the same in every spellbook (white adds its value to the explosion total; orange has no effect).
- v1.9 uses spellbook 1.
- My call: `v1`, `v1.1` and `v1.8` keep their way of naming a component per chip. They have no spellbook field and must load and play unchanged. The builder may express this as one "legacy" spellbook, as long as these versions stay unchanged.

## Colours in spellbook 1

The base value is how far the chip moves. All chips below also move by their value first.

| Colour | Values (price) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| White | 1, 2, 3 | As before. Adds its value to the explosion total. |
| Orange | 1 (3), 6 (22) | No effect. Same in every spellbook. |
| Green | 1 (4), 2 (8), 4 (14) | As before. At round end, each green chip among the last two placed advances the droplet by 0.5. |
| Blue | 1 (5), 2 (10), 4 (19) | New: draws chips and the player picks one (below). |
| Red | 1 (6), 2 (10), 4 (16) | New: extra move by the orange chips in the pot (below). |

The orange 6 chip is new and is in the shop only. Start bag, bonus die and everything else is as in v1.8 (start bag has no blue and no red; the die gives orange 1).

### Red

When a red chip is placed, it counts the orange chips already placed in the pot this round (any orange value; not the bag). It moves its value plus an extra: +1 for one or two orange chips, +2 for three or more, +0 for none.

My call: a red chip does not count itself and does not count other red chips. Orange chips from the bonus die go into the bag and count only after they are placed.

### Blue

When a blue chip is placed, it moves its value first. Then its effect runs:

1. The player draws as many chips from the bag as the value of the blue chip, or fewer if the bag has fewer. These chips are the player's **hand**. Only that player sees the hand.
2. In the next step the player makes a **pick** choice, hidden, at the same time as the other player's Draw or Stop:
   - **Play chip i** of the hand. The chip is placed as a normal draw: it moves, its effect runs, and it counts toward the explosion total if white. This replaces Draw or Stop for that step. The rest of the hand goes back into the bag.
   - **Put all back and Draw**: the whole hand goes back into the bag, then the player draws one random chip as a normal Draw (the returned chips can be drawn).
   - **Put all back and Stop**: the whole hand goes back into the bag, and the player stops brewing.
3. A played blue chip does the same again (a chain), until the bag has no chip left to draw.

Order of events (my call): the played chip is placed, then the rest of the hand returns to the bag, then (if the played chip is blue) the new hand is drawn from that bag. For "put all back and Draw", the hand returns first, then the draw happens. All random draws use the existing seeded chance log, so a replay stays exact.

An empty bag with a non-empty hand does not stop the player: the pick step still happens, and "put all back and Draw" is legal, because the hand refills the bag. The automatic stop for an empty bag applies only when the bag is empty and the player holds no hand. A pick step never comes first in a round, so the rule "the first action of a round is Draw" is untouched.

Blue has no placement bonus in spellbook 1: the v1.8 rule "+1 after a chip with a value other than 1" is gone. Red counts only the player's own pot (also orange chips that came from a hand and were played).

The pick is the only action in its step. The player cannot do Draw or Stop on its own in that step. A player with a hand cannot stop without putting the hand back. If the bag is empty when blue is placed, the hand is empty and there is no pick step. Chips in a hand count as out of the bag; they are not placed and do not count toward the explosion total unless played.

If the other player stops or explodes, the blue player still gets the pick step (simultaneous steps stay as in v1.1).

Because a played chip replaces the Draw, both players have the same number of chips left in the bag after each step, unless a player stopped.

## Shop

The shop items are the chips above at the prices above. "Remove one White 1" is removed (v1.9 has no `remove_chip` item). The chip limits (2 chips per round, different colours) stay. Red is a colour of its own.

## Game record and engine

- The pick is a normal action in the record. A new event for the hand is needed so a replay works. v1, v1.1 and v1.8 records stay unchanged and keep loading.
- Builder: the hand is hidden information. The record is written for replay and keeps the full hand.
- Place bonus of a colour needs the whole pot (red counts orange chips). Today the hook sees only the previous chip. Builder: widen the hook in a way that keeps old spellbooks unchanged.

## Bots

Every bot answers the pick step. My call for the heuristic bot: play the hand chip with the highest advance that does not explode its potion (it knows its own explosion total); if every chip explodes, or the bot's stop rule says stop, put all back and Stop; otherwise put all back and Draw. The random bot picks any legal action.

## CLI

The human CLI shows the hand to its owner only, asks for the pick, and shows the new colours.

## RL environments

The action space and the action mask grow by the pick actions (hand slots 0 to 3 and the two put-back actions). The observation shows the player's own hand. Layout 4 covers all v1.9 changes: the pick actions and the hand, the new colours, orange 6, and the changed shop items and their indices. It is written down in a spec, and the layouts of v1.1 and v1.8 stay checked.

No retrain. A trained policy (PolicyBot, self-play, finetune, offline, evaluate) refuses v1.9 with a clear error (my call), as a v1.8 checkpoint cannot act on layout 4. The check is a short run per environment with the bots and a random policy, as in v1.8.

## Legacy

Notebooks and CLI defaults stay on their pinned version. `tests/test_rules_defaults.py` keeps checking that no default is the newest version.

## Testing the skill

The `rule_changes` skill (`.agents/skills/rule_changes/SKILL.md`) came out of the v1.8 stage. The owner fears it is a documentation burden that goes stale. The test has three parts. Part 1 is in this stage. Parts 2 and 3 are the start of the next stage, because an engineer agent cannot launch a subagent, and the planner in the main session can (my call).

The "small wording fix" is the follow-up that removes the issue numbers and the history sentences from the skill (lines about "what the v1.8 stage really had to touch" and "It worked in v1.8"). It is done first, as a cleanup sub-issue; it does not change what the skill lists.

1. **Fair run (this stage).** The engineers build v1.9 with the skill as it is. The `## Engineer: DONE` comment of every sub-issue of the rule change has a section `### Skill notes`: which files the change really touched, which of them the skill did not list, which steps of the skill were not useful, and what pitfall cost time. The stage review collects them.
2. **Independent review (next stage set-up).** In `/stage-start`, the planner launches a subagent (Opus, max effort) that has not seen this discussion. It reads the skill, the v1.9 diff and the skill notes. It judges what was useful, stale or missing. The owner approved the quota for this.
3. **Rewrite (next stage).** A sub-issue rewrites the skill from 1 and 2. The owner's wish: not a rigid checklist, but shared senior knowledge: the pitfalls we really hit and the general lesson for each, a short way to find the touchpoints (search for the newest ruleset name and the supported-versions list) instead of a fixed file map, and the three rules. The owner judges the result.

An A/B run without the skill is not planned: it costs about double.
